# Founder-Mode Interview Defense: PulseQR

## Core Thesis
**Do not pitch PulseQR as a billion-dollar tech innovation.** Pitch it as a demonstration of extreme agency, 0-to-1 execution, and willingness to do unglamorous sales. 

## The Elevator Pitch (30 Seconds)
"Most PMs just write docs; I actually ship and sell. I built PulseQR, a feedback tool, and sold it to Brew & Beans in Koregaon Park. I drove X scans and Y actionable insights that helped the owner do Z. I'm looking for a Founder's Office role where I can bring that same 0-to-1 hustle to your biggest unowned problems."

## The Interrogation (Top 5 Questions & Winning Answers)

### 1. The Moat Question
**Q:** "QR feedback tools are a dime a dozen with zero moat. Why build this?"
**A:** "The tech wasn't the product; the GTM motion was. I built it as a GTM sandbox to learn B2B ground sales in hospitality. I learned that getting a busy cafe owner to care and adopt is 10x harder than writing the code. The real product was the learning loop: how to pitch an owner, where to place the QR code to get scans, and how to prove ROI to keep them using it."

### 2. The Scale Question
**Q:** "Why only Brew & Beans? Why didn't you scale to 50 or 100 cafes?"
**A:** "Because I learned the hard way about churn and stickiness. Without deep POS integration (like Petpooja or UrbanPiper), standalone tools face high churn risk once the novelty wears off. Scaling a low-retention product before product-market fit is a waste of capital. I paused to apply these exact learnings to a faster-scaling B2B SaaS—like yours."

### 3. The Failure Question
**Q:** "What broke and what did you iterate?"
**A:** "Contextual placement. Table standees had low scan rates initially because diners were immersed in conversations. I realized the user intent to give feedback peaks when paying. Moving the QR prompt to the final bill folder increased scans by 4x. It was a brutal lesson in user context over pure UI design."

### 4. The Ambition Question
**Q:** "If you can build and sell, why do you want to work for me instead of doing your own startup?"
**A:** "I've proven I can do 0 to 1, but I haven't seen 1 to 10 yet. I want to work directly under a founder who has actually built a scaling machine. I bring the grit to handle your messy, unstructured problems, and in return, I get a front-row seat to how a real company is built."

### 5. The Metrics Question
**Q:** "What metric actually mattered for this project?"
**A:** "Not scans or page views. The only metric that mattered was 'Changes Implemented.' If the cafe owner didn't change a menu item, AC setting, or staff behavior based on the data, the product was useless. We intercepted cold brew delays, the owner spoke with barista Pranav, and customer satisfaction rebounded."
